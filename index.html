Expiry Radar was a planned local-first iOS app that would have kept subscription renewals, product warranties, and document / insurance / domain expiries in one list, with photo filing and tiered reminders. It was not built.

Why this shape

  • Subscription tracking alone is a proven category — Bobby, a long-running subscription tracker, has thousands of App Store ratings — but its in-app prices top out around $0.99–$2.99. Nobody has yet shown that a unified list at $9.99 works. That gap is exactly what this page tests, honestly: the price is a test parameter, not a proven one.
  • Reminders and Calendar can hold any single date, for free. The gap this idea targets is maintenance: three separate systems, no photo record attached, and no single “what expires next” view.
  • Everything would stay on your device. No account, no bank linking, no upload of your documents.

Planned capabilities (none of this exists yet)

  • One list for three kinds of expiry: subscription renewals, product warranties, and documents / insurance / domains
  • Photo filing: attach a receipt, warranty card, or document photo to its entry
  • Tiered reminders with a custom lead time per entry, so a passport and a streaming trial do not shout at the same volume
  • Free tier: up to 10 entries with basic reminders
  • Paid unlock (planned): unlimited entries, photo attachments, family sharing, custom lead times

This page promises nothing that is not written above. Features, if the product is built, will follow what registrants actually ask for.

Who this is for

  • People who have been charged for a subscription they meant to cancel, or lost a warranty claim by days
  • Households juggling several warranties, policies, and renewal dates across family members
  • If you track two or three dates and a calendar already covers you, you do not need this

Questions

Can I use it now?
No. Nothing has been built; this page is the whole thing.

Does registering mean I have to buy it?
No. It only means “notify me if it launches”. Decide when there is a real product to look at.

Why not just use Reminders?
You can, and for a few dates it is enough. This idea is for people whose dates, photos, and lead times have outgrown three separate lists kept by hand.

Why local-first?
Document and warranty photos are private. They belong on your device, not on a server built for a validation-stage app.
